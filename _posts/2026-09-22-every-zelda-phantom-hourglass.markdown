Link and Zelda/Tetra (who has reverted to pirate Tetra form) are sailing the seas searching for a legendary ghost ship, and discuss rumours of an "Ocean King" diety protecting the area. They encounter the ghost ship, and Tetra is immediately abducted. Link attempts to jump on board and follow but passes out. He is woken on a beach by a fairy, Ciela.

<p>
Ciela takes Link to see Oshus for more info about the ghost ship. Oshus attempts to dissaude Link from persuing the ghost ship, but Link insists, so he sends Link to meet Linebeck at the harbor. There's an earthquake on the way (and some discussion of frequent recent earthquakes), which has destroyed the bridge to town, so Link has to go another way via a cave. The path is infested by monsters, so Link "acquires" a  sword from Oshus' storehouse. Oshus catches them but relents and gives Link a basic swordfighting lesson. (Wait, isn't this the Link from Wind Waker who's already defeated Ganon once? He doesn't need sword lessons)
</p>

<p>
Link finds his way to the harbor without incident. Linebeck isn't there, or at his favourite haunt, the milk bar. Apparently he's going to the Temple of the Ocean King, which is rumored to suck out the soul of anyone who enters. Link heads there, and immediately finds Linebeck stuck behind some spikes, claiming to have sprained his ankle. Link retracts the spikes (although the temple does indeed slowly drain hearts outside of safe areas), and Linebeck runs to freedom. They meet up by the entrance and Linebeck tells Link that the temple holds a clue to the ghost ship, and gives him a small key he found. Link explores the temple and finds a sea chart for the SW quadrant of the sea.

Back at the harbor, Link meets Oshus and Linebeck at the boat. Oshus prompts Link to scratch off a section of map, revealing an icon over the Isle of Ember where a fortune teller, Astrid lives. Oshus suggests that Link go see her, Linebeck agrees to sail there, and Ciela agrees to come along too (which is convenient as we need a cursor).

After a quick debrief with Astrid, she tells the gang they need to collect another 2 spirits, and for now to return to the Temple of the Ocean King. Once there, Oshus pops up and tells Link to do up to the dais which we ran past earlier. The hourglass there starts glowing and Link grabs it - it's the titular Phantom Hourglass.

## General Review

I have a lot of nostalgia for Phantom Hourglass but it's definitely a flawed game and some parts of it are rough.

One of the most notable aspects of PH is that it is a DS game, gratuitously so. It uses every unusual hardware feature of the DS at every opportunity. Of course it's all touch controls, which works *ok*. Touch based swordfighting isn't great, but some of the items, like the boomerang and bombchus, are really fun twists on classic zelda items. More than that though, there's stuff like using the microphone to blow out flames or shout, and that one infamous puzzle where the solution is to physically close the DS. It ends up feeling a bit gimmicky at times.

Also the game constantly pauses in dungeons to show you cutscenes of doors opening, bridges extending, etc whenever you do any puzzle stuff, which gets really old really fast. This is doubly unnecessary as the map also shows you a marker for whatever just happened so you don't even really need to see it. At the very least they should have sped these up about 2-3x faster.

The sailing in the game does kinda drag after a while. I didn't mind when I was just playing through the story, but when it came to trying to get 100% it really started to drag. It's just kinda slow and boring to keep revisiting islands. Also ship customisation is a real letdown. It only changes how much HP your ship has, which is barely relevant. If they had actually let you make the ship faster it would have been so much better.

I think my biggest criticism of PH is that it feels very handholdy, especially in the first half of the game or so. Things are constantly explained and reexplained and you are never expected to think for yourself before someone pops up to tell you exactly what to do. For example, there are many puzzles in the game that revolve around drawing on or otherwise notating the map, which is a fun mechanic and decent use of the DS touch screen. But almost every time you need to do this the game will explicitly tell you to do so. This was a general problem in this era of Nintendo design, isn't the worst Zelda game for this problem (that's Skyward Sword) but it still grates on me a lot.

The story is decent, but it commits the crime of relegating Tetra, one of the most memorable and interesting Zelda incarnations, to being a useless damsel in distress, in fact just a statue for most of the game. She's barely in it outside of the very beginning and end. This is also not just a direct sequel but actually has the same Link and Zelda(Tetra) as Windwaker which is quite unusual for Zelda.

No super memorable dungeons again, in fact the dungeons tend to suffer a bit because you have a very high fidelity map of them as soon as you enter and constantly visible on the second screen. The most infamous one is the Temple of the Ocean King, which you repeatedly revisit and which has a unique "timed semi-stealth based" vibe rather than a normal Zelda dungeon design. I actually didn't dislike this overmuch, the fact that each time you come back to it you have new abilities to take new paths keeps me engaged enough, and it's not so long as to become boring especially when you get the midway checkpoint.

## Sidquests and Stuff

As I alluded to earlier, I didn't 100% the game. This is what I had left:

* 3 Heart Containers (from Archery (very difficult), the trading quest, and purchasing from Masked Beedle)
* A handful of spirit gems
* 10 treasure maps and the associated ship parts and sand

A bit of a bad showing for minigames this time. The ship shooting range is a boring touchscreen spam fest. The archery minigame is brutally hard to get the top score on, requiring basically perfect play (I only managed 1950/2000, probably could have got there with enough grinding). There's the "Goron Game" which is very simple, and the Maze Island which is mainly an exercise in finding a decent route around a not-super-interesting maze.

The spirit gems are a major collectible, but they feel really underwhelming as there are so many of them and you need so many to get any actual reward. Finding all of them is also annoying as there are so many to keep track of and no help in game to find them. No heart pieces this time, only full containers, which means they are very rare and only come as "big" rewards for big sidquests, which in turn become disappointing compared to an actual unique reward.

When at sea, there's fishing, which is fun enough although you have to sail around to find fishing spots which is a drag (see above). And salvaging which is a very annoying minigame with bad controls, disappointing rewards, and is really annoying if you break the salvage arm as you have to go back to the start island and pay every time.
